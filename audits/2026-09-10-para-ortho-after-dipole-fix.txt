9. CLOSING THE STEP CONVERGENCE
==================================================================

9a. The cause, read off CREM_SKIP_CENSUS.  Under the default
    stochasticElectricDipole model the orbit does NOT decay between
    photons -- the semi-major axis is flat to five digits for nine
    consecutive checkpoints, then one photon moves it by a factor of
    four (para index 2, master seed 42, s_max=0.30):

      #    t(ps)        a(m)        L(hbar)
      1     0.0000   1.0584e-10   1.00000
      5    63.4899   1.0583e-10   1.00000
      9   126.9774   1.0583e-10   1.00001
     10   142.8489   2.7439e-11   0.50944   <- photon
     15   144.2317   2.7435e-11   0.50955
     27   147.5482   2.7427e-11   0.50950

    Six sites nevertheless applied the sinking-envelope shape
    u(n)=u0(1-s)^(-2/3) within each skip: the checkpoint's elapsed
    proper time (1-s/2), the hazard's integralFactor, the identity's
    meanInSkipGrowth and its envelope reference, the photon's position
    in the skip and its quantum (energyRatio), and a sixth, duplicated
    copy of the elapsed-time formula in the lab-frame tail.

9b. Effect on the collapse time, one trajectory (para index 2, seed 42):

      s_max    before      after
      0.30     147.824     199.428
      0.20     163.715     198.970
      0.10     180.782     198.944
      0.045    191.427     199.747

    Before: monotonic, +29.5% and still climbing at the finest step.
    After:  flat to 0.4% over a 6.7x range of step size.

9c. The photon events themselves are now step-independent.  Times and
    semi-major axes at the two emissions, s_max=0.30 against 0.10:

      photon 1   t=186.7296 ps vs 186.7084 ps   a: 1.0583e-10 -> 3.528e-11
      photon 2   t=199.1727 ps vs 198.6764 ps   a: 3.527e-11  -> 7.91e-12

    0.011% and 0.25% apart, against 22% before.

9d. The convergence table recorded in the code at maximumJumpParameter
    (124.169 / 124.957 / 125.585 / 124.986 / 124.328 ps for s_max
    0.012..0.30, claimed flat to +/-0.6%) reproduces at no
    configuration this file has today.  It predates the present
    defaults and was carried forward unremeasured, which is how a real

11. CLOSING THE L<->S EXCHANGE

11a. Where the damage was.  CREM_LS_BALANCE prints, per half-step,
     the change in |L| and the energy that change would demand
     (dE = omega dL on the circular family) beside the dipole coupling
     energy that would have to pay it.  Over the 122 half-steps of a
     default trajectory (seed 42, para) the damage is entirely in the
     half-step that FOLLOWS a photon, and scales with how far that
     photon moved the orbit:

       orbit change       dL/L      h^2/(Aa) after
       a x 0.333          0.0003        1.0006
       a x 0.224          0.0185        1.0373
       a x 0.130          1.1886        4.7898

     Every other half-step moves L by 1e-5..1e-4 relative.  At the last
     one the exchange demanded 1673.9 eV against 0.0449 eV of coupling
     energy, a factor of 37000.

11b. The cause is the operator split, not the coupling.  The symmetric
     split gives each half 0.5*checkpointProperTime on the assumption
     that the radiation between them is a perturbation.  One photon
     here takes the semi-major axis down by a factor of eight and the
     orbital period with it by twenty, so a second half sized for the
     OLD orbit runs the new one for twenty times the precession it
     should see.  The second half now gets the proper time that
     actually follows the last photon, which the lab-frame block
     already computes; with no photon it is 0.5*checkpointProperTime
     and the split is unchanged.

11c. Two closures tried first, both worse, both recorded in the code:
     limiting each half-step's change in |L| to couplingEnergy/omega
     (an energy used as a per-step allowance, so its total scales with
     the checkpoint count: 216.73/210.96/204.93 ps for s_max
     0.30/0.20/0.10), and clamping |L| to the circular value (one-sided,
     so it ratchets: 216.69/210.92/204.90 ps, and the first photon's
     time moves 6.5% where it had been steady to 0.011%).

13. WHAT WOULD TURN CONTACT INTO A RATE
==================================================================

13a. The rate factorizes, and the factorization is exact.  For the
     singlet channel

       Gamma = 4 * (pi r_e^2 c) * <n(0)>

     with sigma*v = pi r_e^2 c = 7.478839e-21 m^3/s and
     <n(0)> = |psi(0)|^2 = 1/(pi a_Ps^3) = 2.685077e+29 1/m^3 this gives
     Gamma = 8.032503e+09 1/s, i.e. tau = 124.4942 ps against the
     measured 124.49 ps.  So there are exactly three factors and no
     free parameter.

13b. Which of the three the model can own.
       sigma*v = pi r_e^2 c   CLASSICAL IN FORM: the geometric disc of
                              the classical electron radius times c.
                              The model carries r_e and c already.
       the factor 4           spin statistics, the singlet channel.
                              An import, but a counting one.
       <n(0)>                 THE DENSITY OF THE RELATIVE COORDINATE AT
                              CONTACT.  This is what the model cannot
                              supply, and the reason is structural.

13c. Why it is structural, measured rather than argued.  A finite n(0)
     needs the orbit to pass through the origin, i.e. L -> 0 and e -> 1.
     Classical radiation reaction does the OPPOSITE: it circularizes.
     Started from the sampled band (CREM_INITIAL_BAND=1, seed 42, para)
     the eccentricity falls monotonically through the cascade:

       t(ps)      a(m)         e         L(hbar)
         0.000  1.1159e-10   0.078766   1.02365
       214.845  3.7855e-11   0.035280   0.59769
       229.336  8.7045e-12   0.029940   0.28666
       229.388  8.6984e-12   0.003764   0.28668
       229.440  8.6924e-12   0.000000   0.28687   <- and zero thereafter

     Fitted over the first two photons, e ~ a^0.74.  So the model's own
     dynamics drives the orbit AWAY from the only configuration that
     would have contact.  The channel is not small here; it is exactly
     closed, and radiation closes it.

13d. And the orbit stops short of the contact radius anyway: the
     smallest separation reached is a = 2.718e-14 m = 9.6 r_e, so the
     volume r < r_e is never entered and the density there is zero, not
     merely small.

13e. So the answer to "how to get a rate" is one import, and a precise
     one: <n(0)>.  Not a mechanism, not a coupling, not a
     cross-section -- the single quantum fact that a bound state has
     finite amplitude at zero separation while a classical orbit, being
     a curve, has none.  Supply that one number and the rest of the
     rate is classical and comes out at 124.4942 ps.

15. LETTING THE DYNAMICS CHOOSE THE CHANNEL
==================================================================

15a. The rule.  Momentum conservation makes a two-photon final state
     back to back, defining one axis; each photon has helicity +-1
     along that axis, so the pair can carry projection 0 or +-2 but
     never +-1.  A J=1 state therefore cannot go to two photons
     (Landau-Yang).  Implemented as: round |J| = |L_orb + S1 + S2| to
     the nearest integer and refuse two photons exactly when it is 1.
     annihilationPhotonEnergiesFor no longer sees selectedPhenomenon.

15b. Result, 12 trajectories per channel, master seed 42:

       prepared   2 gamma   3 gamma   |J| range        agrees with
                                                       its preparation
       para          7         5      [0.208, 0.738]      7/12 = 58%
       ortho         0        12      [0.917, 1.092]     12/12 = 100%

15c. How to read that.  ORTHO IS NOW A RESULT, not a label, and it
     comes out right on every trajectory: the anti-aligned
     configuration is the stable fixed point, |S1+S2| stays at 1 hbar
     to 14 digits, and with L_orb at 0.095 hbar the total rounds to 1
     every time.

     PARA IS RIGHT 58% OF THE TIME, and the other 42% is not a bug in
     the gate -- it is the model's known destruction of the singlet,
     now visible in the OBSERVABLE instead of hidden in the internal
     state.  The aligned configuration is the unstable fixed point, so
     by the time the pair annihilates it no longer has S=0, and the
     selection rule correctly refuses two photons for it.  Before this
     change the model emitted 2 gamma regardless and the defect did
     not appear in the output at all.

15d. WHAT IS STILL IMPORTED, and it has to be said plainly.  |J| comes
     out at 0.208, 0.738, 0.917, 1.092 -- classical continuous values,
     not integers.  Rounding them is the assumption that J is
     QUANTIZED, and that is an import, the same kind as the three the
     README already lists.  What conservation supplies for free is only
     the implication "J=1 cannot go to two photons"; deciding that a
     state with |J|=0.74 IS a J=1 state is not conservation, it is
     quantization.  So this replaces a strong import (the channel
     itself, taken from the command line) with a weaker one, rather
     than removing an import.

15e. Output change to be aware of: the leading-photon panel now mixes
     the two multiplicities within one --phenomenon batch, since the
     multiplicity varies trajectory by trajectory.  That is the
     mixture becoming visible, not a plotting fault.

     CREM_CHANNEL_FROM_FLAG restores the old behaviour for comparison.
     make validation 55/55 with the gate active.

16. REMOVING THE QUANTIZATION IMPORT FROM THE CHANNEL RULE

16a. The problem with the first version.  It rounded |J| to an integer,
     and |J| comes out at 0.208, 0.738, 0.917, 1.092 -- continuous
     classical values.  Calling a state with |J|=0.74 a J=1 state is

17. MAKING THE CHANNEL DETERMINISTIC
==================================================================

17a. What does NOT work: a geometric criterion on the orientation.
     A two-photon state carries projection 0 or +-2 hbar on its own
     axis, and projection 0 is achievable for ANY J by taking the axis
     perpendicular to J.  So 'does a permitted axis exist' forbids
     nothing and cannot discriminate.  Landau-Yang is not a condition
     on one projection; it is the vanishing of the whole amplitude for
     J=1 on EVERY axis, which is why it has to enter through the
     magnitude rather than the geometry.

17b. What does work: read the weight where the orientation is still
     exact.  At PREPARATION spin quantization fixes cos = +-1, so
     w_2g is exactly 1 or exactly 0 and the draw is degenerate --
     the channel is deterministic with no threshold and no rounding:

       para  idx 2,3,5   w_2g = 1   ->  2 photons
       ortho idx 2,3,5   w_2g = 0   ->  3 photons

     That is now the default.  The reason is not convenience: the drift
     away from cos = +-1 is a known defect of this model rather than
     physics.  Real positronium conserves S, so the annihilating state
     IS the prepared one; the classical pair loses the singlet because
     two classical vectors cannot represent an entangled one.  Putting
     that artefact into the photon multiplicity would place a model
     failure into an observable.

17c. The terminal reading is kept as a probe, CREM_CHANNEL_AT_ANNIHILATION.
     There para's weight spreads over [0.43, 0.94] instead of sitting at
     1 and the channel has to be drawn.  It measures how far the singlet
     has been destroyed; it is not a branching-ratio prediction.

17d. The rule degrades gracefully.  With --no-spin-quantization the
     PREPARED configuration is itself intermediate and the draw is
     genuinely needed.  Measured weights: para 0.763, 0.903, 0.910;
     ortho 0.067, 0.121, 0.706 -- i.e. the sampled band does not
     produce clean ortho, which is the same conclusion this project
     already records for the band.

18. IS THE LOSS OF THE SINGLET A MODEL DEFECT OR PHYSICS?
==================================================================

The claim under test was: the drift of the mutual angle is a defect of
this model rather than physics, because real positronium conserves S
and a classical pair cannot represent an entangled singlet.  The
CONCLUSION survives; the MECHANISM I gave for it was wrong.

18a. The quantum side holds, and more strongly than I stated it.
     H_dd ~ [S1.S2 - 3(S1.n)(S2.n)]/r^3 has a scalar part (k=0) and a
     tensor part (k=2).  Wigner-Eckart kills both for S=0 -> S=1:

       transition          k   permitted S'   result
       singlet->triplet    0        0         VANISHES
       singlet->triplet    2        2         VANISHES
       triplet->triplet  0,2      1..3        allowed

     Two spin-1/2 have no S'=2, so the dipole-dipole interaction CANNOT
     mix singlet and triplet.  Not 'S is conserved in general' but: the
     very interaction that moves the angle classically is forbidden
     from moving it quantum mechanically.

     Caveat worth stating: the Zeeman term is rank 1 and DOES mix S=0
     with S=1,m=0 -- that is the measured magnetic quenching of
     ortho-Ps, and this model has --external-field.  A consistency
     note: for positronium mu1+mu2 ~ S1-S2, so the channel weight is
     exactly the operator that performs that mixing.

18b. The classical mechanism is derivable and explains the asymmetry.
     gamma_2 = -gamma_1, and the partner's dipole field satisfies
     B1 = -B2 for anti-aligned moments, B1 = +B2 for aligned.  So
       ortho: omega_1 = gamma_1 B_1 = -gamma_1 B_2 = omega_2
              -> both spins precess about the SAME vector, angle
                 preserved identically;
       para:  omega_1 = -omega_2
              -> the spins counter-rotate, angle changes.

18c. CORRECTION: it is a LIBRATION, not a drift.  Measured over 63
     checkpoints, seed 42:

       channel  start    min      max      end      span
       para     +1.000   +0.322   +1.000   +0.800   0.678
       ortho    -1.000   -1.000   -1.000   -1.000   0.000

     Para's cosine RETURNS TO 1 repeatedly -- it oscillates within a
     bounded range rather than running away.  And started from a
     sampled angle instead of the extremum the change goes BOTH WAYS in
     both channels: para 3 up / 5 down, ortho 4 up / 4 down.  So
     'always downward, the aligned configuration is unstable' was an
     artefact of starting exactly at the MAXIMUM of the oscillation,
     from which the first excursion can only go down.

18d. What survives.  The conclusion for the channel rule stands and is
     better founded: the terminal angle is a PHASE of that libration at
     the moment the trajectory stops, aliased by the checkpoint spacing
     (the precession period deep in the orbit is ~5e-6 ps against a
     0.023 ps checkpoint), so it measures nothing.  Reading the weight
     at preparation is right -- because the terminal value is a random
     phase, not because the singlet was destroyed along the way.

19. WHEN CAN EACH CHANNEL EMIT, FROM THE MOMENT CONFIGURATION
==================================================================

The model has two radiative channels during the inspiral and one at the
end, and the moment configuration enters exactly two of the three.

19a. E1, the electric dipole: DOES NOT depend on the moments at all.
     It comes from the charge separation d = q_eff r.  Measured over 63
     checkpoints, seed 42:

       channel   E1 min [W]     E1 max [W]
       para      1.1673e-08     3.8455e-04
       ortho     1.1673e-08     3.7804e-04

     Identical at the start and 1.7% apart at the end, which is the
     trajectories having diverged, not a channel difference.  This is
     the channel that actually fires every inspiral photon, and it is
     why the two collapse times differ by only 1.3e-4.

19b. M1, the magnetic dipole: depends on NOTHING ELSE.  It radiates
     from the coherent sum m = mu1 + mu2, so P_M1 ~ |m|^2:

       channel   |m|/mu           M1 share min    M1 share max
       para      1.626 .. 2.000   2.7392e-24      2.2375e-10
       ortho     0.000 .. 0.000   1.1532e-48      7.5789e-43

     PARA CAN EMIT M1, ORTHO CANNOT.  The two maxima differ by 32
     orders of magnitude, and ortho's residue is roundoff: |m| prints
     as exactly 0.000000e+00 at every checkpoint, and since both spins
     precess about the same vector (omega_1 = omega_2, section 18) the
     time derivative d(mu1+mu2)/dt vanishes identically too.

     So ortho's inability to radiate M1 is DYNAMICALLY PROTECTED, not a
     coincidence of preparation.  Para's ability varies over the
     libration -- |m|/mu runs 2.000 down to 1.626, a factor 1.51 in
     power -- but never vanishes.

19c. BUT NEITHER EVER ACTUALLY FIRES AN M1 PHOTON.  Para's M1 share
     peaks at 2.2e-10 of the radiated power.  The emission hazard is
     E1's throughout, so the moment configuration decides whether M1 is
     ALLOWED, not whether a photon is emitted.

19d. Annihilation: the same quantity decides the multiplicity.
     w_2g = |mu1+mu2|^2/(|mu1|+|mu2|)^2, so aligned moments give 1 and
     two photons, anti-aligned give 0 and three.

     That is the structural point worth carrying: ONE quantity,
     |mu1 + mu2|, gates both the M1 channel during the inspiral and the
     two-photon channel at the end.  It is the model's single mechanism
     with the shape of the C-parity selection rule, and it acts in both
     places for the same reason -- coherent cancellation of the two
     moments when they are anti-aligned.

20. DOES THE MODEL CONSERVE C-PARITY?
==================================================================

Three separate questions hide in that one, and they have three
different answers.

20a. IS THE DYNAMICS C-INVARIANT?  Yes, by construction.  C exchanges
     e- and e+, which on the model's quantities is q1 <-> q2, r -> -r,
     and mu -> -mu (since mu = gamma S and gamma ~ q, while the spin
     stays with the particle).  Term by term:

       quantity                             C   why
       q1 q2 / r          (Coulomb)         +   q1q2 unchanged
       d = q1 r1 + q2 r2  (E1 source)       -   d -> -d
       |d''|^2            (E1 power)        +   square of a C-odd
       mu1.mu2, (mu1.n)(mu2.n)              +   two C-odd factors
       m = mu1 + mu2      (M1 source)       -   m -> -m
       |m''|^2            (M1 power)        +   square
       q (mu2-mu1).(v x n)  (spin-orbit)    +   C-odd charge TIMES C-odd moment
       omega = gamma B    (BMT rate)        +   C-odd times C-odd
       dmu/dt = omega x mu                  -   consistent with mu being C-odd
       w_2g = |m|^2/(2mu)^2 (channel)       +   square

     Every observable comes out C-even, and every C-odd quantity
     reaches an observable only squared or paired with a charge.

20b. ARE THE STATES C EIGENSTATES?  No, and this is the real answer.
     In QED the +-1 comes from the EXCHANGE SYMMETRY of the
     wavefunction -- the singlet spin function is antisymmetric, the
     triplet symmetric -- and a classical configuration of two vectors
     has no exchange symmetry to be +-1.  There is nothing to conserve.

     The signature is measurable.  A C eigenstate must have zero
     expectation for every C-odd observable, and m = mu1 + mu2 is
     C-odd.  The model's para configuration has |m| = 2 mu, the largest
     value it can take.  So the classical para state FAILS the
     necessary condition for being a C eigenstate.  Ortho passes it
     (m = 0) but for the wrong reason: the vector itself vanishes
     rather than an expectation over a symmetric state.

20c. SO WHAT CARRIES THE SELECTION RULE?  Not a parity label -- the
     vanishing of a C-odd amplitude.  m is exactly the operator that
     connects C=+1 to C=-1 (for positronium m ~ S1-S2, which maps the
     singlet onto the m=0 triplet), and the model makes it vanish
     identically for ortho.  That is the right shape, reached without
     any parity quantum number.

20d. WHERE THE ANALOGY BREAKS, QUANTITATIVELY.  This is the part worth
     recording against the model:

       state      QM <m>   QM <m^2>/(g hbar)^2   classical |m|^2
       singlet      0              3                   1
       triplet      0              1                   0

     Quantum mechanically <m> vanishes in BOTH states, and <m^2>
     differs only by a factor of 3.  Classically |m|^2 is 1 for para
     and EXACTLY 0 for ortho, a ratio of infinity.  The model's exact
     cancellation therefore has no quantum counterpart: the triplet's
     magnetic-moment fluctuation does not vanish, it is merely three
     times smaller than the singlet's.  The model reproduces the SHAPE
     of the C selection rule through a quantity that, quantum
     mechanically, does not vanish where the model makes it vanish.

21. THE 1S E1 OBJECTION, CHECKED AGAINST THIS MODEL
==================================================================

The objection: positronium in 1S (L=0) cannot radiate E1, because
1S -> 1S with a photon is forbidden by angular momentum conservation,
Delta L = +-1.  Checked here, it lands harder than stated.

21a. THE MODEL NEVER OCCUPIES 1S, so the prohibition does not apply to
     the state it actually has.  Measured, L before the first emission
     is 1.0000 hbar, not 0: the ground configuration is the
     Bohr-Sommerfeld circular orbit n=1, k=1, which carries L = hbar.
     In quantum terms that angular momentum belongs to a P state, and a
     P state radiating E1 to an S state is allowed.  The model evades
     the objection by not being in 1S.

21b. BUT THE EVASION COSTS MORE THAN IT SAVES.  The model's own probe
     reports, for every photon, the angular momentum the photon demands
     and the amount the orbit actually surrenders:

       photon   n_eff    L before   demands hbar   surrenders   short   harmonic
         1     1.0000    1.0000        1.0000        0.4227    0.5773      1
         2     0.5773    0.5775        1.0000        0.3042    0.6958      1
         3     0.2724    0.2737        1.0000        0.1787    0.8213      1
         4     0.0815    0.1738        1.0000        0.1578    0.8422      1

     Every photon is harmonic 1, i.e. E1, and demands exactly one hbar.
     The orbit gives up between 0.42 and 0.16 of it.  The shortfall runs
     0.58 to 0.84 hbar per photon and GROWS down the cascade.

21c. WHY: THE MODEL QUANTIZES THE ENERGY AND NOT THE ANGULAR MOMENTUM.
     Energy leaves in discrete photons of hbar*omega, but the angular
     momentum is removed by the CLASSICAL law, angularExponent =
     -(1-e^2)/(2+e^2), which at e=0 is -1/2, i.e. L ~ |E|^(-1/2) for a
     circular orbit.  Verified against the measured ratios:

       photon   E ratio   L ratio measured   E^(-1/2)   agreement
         1      2.9999       0.577348        0.577361     1.0000
         2      4.4576       0.473278        0.473639     0.9992
         3      7.6954       0.349864        0.360482     0.9705

     The first three hit the classical law to four figures.  So the two
     bookkeepings are inconsistent with each other: a quantum of energy
     hbar*omega must carry a quantum of angular momentum, and here it
     does not.

21d. THE SHARPEST FORM OF THE OBJECTION.  L before the first emission is
     1.0000 hbar.  If the photon took its hbar, the orbit would land at
     L = 0 -- exactly the 1S state -- and further E1 would be forbidden,
     which is the objection in its original form.  The model removes
     0.4227 hbar instead and emits three more photons.  The entire
     cascade beyond the first photon exists only because angular
     momentum is not conserved photon by photon.

21e. Status.  The README already declares that 93.4% of the collapse
     runs below n=1 where no ladder exists, so the REGIME is a known
     declared limitation.  What is added here is the mechanism and its
     size: it is not merely that the ladder is absent, it is that the
     emission removes a fraction of hbar per photon, by a classical law,
     while charging a full quantum of energy for it.

24. LOOKING FOR THE MISSING VERTEX

24a. The model already reproduces alpha^5, the power of the 2-gamma

       lambdabar_C / c                = 1.288089e-21 s

       this model's collapse time     = 3.2040 x alpha^-5 lambdabar_C/c
       ratio                          = 1.6021

     So the classical inspiral is not off by powers of alpha from the
     annihilation rate -- it is off by 1.6.

24b. And alpha itself is IN the model, as its own orbital velocity:

       measured v/c at a_pair = 0.003648676
       alpha/2                = 0.003648676   ratio 1.000000

     That is not a coincidence, it is the Bohr relation v/c = alpha/n
     evaluated on the orbit the model actually prepares.

24c. So BOTH factors of the 1/1114 are present as measured quantities:

       Ore-Powell phase space, from the model's own spectrum  0.123023857
       2 x (v/c at a_pair), i.e. alpha                        0.007297353
       product                                          8.977485e-04 = 1/1113.9
       physical 4(pi^2-9)alpha/(9 pi)                   8.977485e-04 = 1/1113.9

24d. THIS IS NOT A DERIVATION, and the weak point is worth stating
     plainly.  v/c is not a constant in this model -- it grows down the
     orbit:

       radius                v/c        2(v/c)/alpha   phase x 2(v/c)
       a_pair (start)        0.003649       1.00        1/1113.9
       0.05 a_pair           0.016317       4.47        1/249.1
       collision boundary    0.051600      14.14        1/78.8
       Compton barrier       0.085292      23.38        1/47.7

     Only at the STARTING radius does 2(v/c) equal alpha, because
     v/c = alpha/n is the Bohr relation and holds on the Bohr orbit.
     Choosing which v/c is therefore choosing the answer.  And knowing
     to multiply the two factors at all is precisely the QED content.

24e. CONCLUSION: the vertex is not a missing NUMBER -- both numbers are
     already here -- it is a missing RULE.  The rule is that one more
     photon costs one more power of the coupling.  Classical radiation
     theory has no such rule: a classical source emits any number of
     photons with no per-photon suppression, which is exactly what a
     vertex factor encodes and exactly what this model cannot supply.
     That is why two independent routes -- asking what turns contact
     into a rate, and decomposing the Ore-Powell suppression -- both
     land on the same single missing alpha.

    spacing.  What follows measures where the photons actually land.

     scale.

32. STEP 0 OF THE FREEING PLAN: DOES THE ORBIT-TIED ZPF BAND ADMIT A
    DISTINGUISHED RADIUS?

    The concern raised when the plan was proposed: the zero-point field's
    band is tied to the orbital frequency ([0.5, 2] x omega_orb, 8 modes)
    and rides up with the orbit.  A field that looks the same at every
    radius cannot select one, and step 4 would be dead before starting.

32a. THE AMPLITUDE LAW IS THE CORRECT BROADBAND ONE, AND THAT DISPOSES OF
     HALF THE CONCERN.  makeZeroPointField sets amplitude = C*omega_orb^2
     with

       C = scale*sqrt(2 hbar (f_hi^4 - f_lo^4)/(8 pi^2 c^3 N eps0)),

     i.e. u = eps0<E^2> = hbar(f_hi^4-f_lo^4) omega_orb^4/(8 pi^2 c^3).
     That is exactly the integral of rho(omega) = hbar omega^3/(2 pi^2 c^3)
     over [f_lo, f_hi]*omega_orb.  Because the RELATIVE bandwidth is
     constant, a real broadband omega^3 field sampled at the orbital
     frequency gives the same omega^4 energy scaling.  The band-riding is
     a restriction to the part of the spectrum that acts, not a distortion
     of how it scales.

     What band-riding does still give up is the off-resonant spectrum and
     the phase history a fixed field would carry.  Those matter for the
     dynamics, not for whether a scale exists.

32b. THE FIELD'S GRIP ON THE ORBIT IS NOT SCALE-FREE.  Sampled directly
     (4096 phase points per radius, rms):

       a/a_pair   E_zpf (V/m)   E_bind (V/m)   ratio       r/lambda_C
       100        1.3899e+02    1.2856e+07     1.0811e-05  27407.2
       10         1.3903e+05    1.2856e+09     1.0814e-04   2740.7
       1          1.3915e+08    1.2856e+11     1.0824e-03    274.1
       0.1        1.3953e+11    1.2856e+13     1.0854e-02     27.4
       0.01       1.4071e+14    1.2856e+15     1.0946e-01      2.74
       0.001      1.4411e+17    1.2856e+17     1.1210e+00      0.274
       0.0001     1.5096e+20    1.2856e+19     1.1743e+01      0.027

     The ratio rises exactly as r^-1 (one decade per decade) and crosses 1
     near a/a_pair = 8.9e-4, i.e. r = 0.244 lambda_C.

32c. VERDICT ON STEP 0: THE BAND DOES ADMIT A DISTINGUISHED RADIUS, so
     step 4 is alive and the power balance is worth measuring.

     But the radius the FIELD ratio picks out is 0.244 lambda_C -- the
     Compton scale, for the fourth time, after mu/(ec) (30a), the
     frequency-ratio peak at 0.726 lambda_C (30b) and the stationary
     harmonic at 0.726 lambda_C (31c).

     This is a field-strength crossing, NOT the power balance.  Absorption
     is second order in a weak field while radiation is set by the orbit,
     so the two conditions sit at different radii and only the second one
     is the SED scale.  Measuring it is in progress.

33. STEP 4, FIRST ATTEMPT: THE BALANCE MEASUREMENT AS DESIGNED DOES NOT
    WORK, AND WHY

    Design: integrate the same circular orbit twice with the full engine,
    field on and field off, difference the final mechanical energies, and
    call the difference the ZPF contribution.  Scan the radius for a sign
    change.

33a. WHAT IT APPEARED TO SHOW.  20 orbits, 3 field seeds averaged:

       a/a_pair   r/lambda_C   dE_off (eV)   ZPF part (eV)   |ZPF/rad|
       1           274.07      +3.395e-05    -1.333e-01       3925
       0.3          82.22      -5.713e-03    -2.000e+00        350
       0.1          27.41      -1.401e-01    -1.214e+01         86.6
       0.03          8.22      -1.723e+00    -1.796e+02        104.3
       0.01          2.74      -5.763e+00    +1.834e+02         31.8
       0.003         0.82      numerical failure with the field on
       0.001         0.27      numerical failure with the field on
       0.0003        0.08      numerical failure with the field on

     A sign change between 0.03 and 0.01 a_pair, which is exactly the
     signature a balance radius would leave.  It is not one.

33b. THREE DISCRIMINATORS, TWO OF WHICH KILL IT.  At r = 0.1 a_pair:

     (a) Scale dependence.  Absorption from a weak field must go as
         scale^2, so contribution/scale^2 must be flat:

           scale    ZPF part (eV)   part/scale^2
           1          -3.811e+01      -3.811e+01
           0.5        -4.130e+01      -1.652e+02
           0.25       -1.299e+01      -2.079e+02
           0.125      -5.461e+00      -3.495e+02

         It varies ninefold, and the RAW contribution is larger at scale
         0.5 than at scale 1 -- non-monotonic in field strength.  This is
         not an absorption law; the response is not perturbative anywhere
         in the range tested.

     (b) Seed spread.  Six field seeds at scale 1, same radius, same
         everything else: +1.286, +3.210, +3.331, +1.788, +1.658 eV --
         against the -38.11 eV that seed 1000 gave in (a).  The sign is
         set by the seed.  The three-seed means in 33a therefore mean
         nothing.

33c. THE DESIGN FLAW, NAMED.  Differencing the INSTANTANEOUS mechanical
     energies of two trajectories that have diverged.  The band rides with
     the orbit, so the drive is resonant by construction and the
     perturbation accumulates; after 20 orbits the field-on and field-off
     runs sit at different orbital phases, and the endpoint difference
     measures that phase difference, not a secular drift.  Nothing about
     the sign change in 33a survives this.

33d. THE FIX.  Measure the ZPF work directly along ONE trajectory,
     integral of (q1 E.v1 + q2 E.v2) dt, or fit the secular trend of the
     orbit-averaged energy.  A work integral is immune to divergence
     between two runs because there is only one run.  That is what step 4
     must be rebuilt on.

33e. ONE RESULT THAT DOES SURVIVE.  At scale 1 the field destroys the
     orbit outright below about 0.003 a_pair (numerical failure with the
     field on, clean without it, at all three radii tested).  That is
     consistent with 32b: the ZPF field passes the binding field near
     0.244 lambda_C = 8.9e-4 a_pair, and an orbit cannot survive a
     transverse field stronger than the one binding it.  So the model's
     ZPF has a floor below which it cannot be run at physical scale, and
     that floor is again the Compton scale.

34. STEP 4 REBUILT: THE METHOD IS NOW RIGHT, THE STATISTICS ARE NOT

    Section 33 named the flaw -- differencing instantaneous energies of two
    diverged trajectories -- and the fix: measure along ONE trajectory.

34a. THE REBUILT MEASUREMENT.  Without the field, mechanical + radiated +
     Schott energy is conserved, and the engine records all three at every
     frame.  With the field on, the DRIFT of that same sum is the work the
     field did.  One run, nothing to diverge from, and the field-off
     residual is the method's own noise floor, printed beside every number
     it validates.

     r = 1 a_pair, 20 orbits, 400 frames: noise floor 4.77e-4 eV against a
     first four-seed mean of 1.30e-2 eV, a factor 27 above it.  The sign
     is now POSITIVE, i.e. the field pumps in, which is what the module
     says it does and the opposite of what section 33a reported.  The
     systematic artefact is gone.

34b. TIME SCALING SAYS THE MEAN IS NOT CONVERGED.  Four seeds, r = a_pair:

       orbits   ZPF work (eV)   work/orbit    work/orbit^2
         5        -9.765e-03    -1.953e-03     -3.906e-04
        10        +1.661e-02    +1.661e-03     +1.661e-04
        20        +1.299e-02    +6.495e-04     +3.247e-05
        40        +3.116e-02    +7.789e-04     +1.947e-05

     The sign flips between 5 and 10 orbits, and neither work/orbit
     (secular pumping) nor work/orbit^2 (resonant growth) is constant.
     Four seeds cannot tell them apart.

34c. THE SPREAD, MEASURED.  24 field seeds, r = a_pair, 20 orbits:

       n = 24          11 positive, 13 negative
       mean            +1.4810e-02 eV
       median          -7.3694e-04 eV      OPPOSITE SIGN
       sigma            7.8719e-02 eV
       standard error   1.6069e-02 eV      mean/error = 0.92
       largest value   +3.7625e-01 eV      4.6 sigma
       mean without that one seed  -9.0510e-04 eV   SIGN FLIPS

     The mean is within one standard error of zero, the median has the
     opposite sign to the mean, and deleting the single 4.6-sigma seed
     flips the mean.  At the Bohr radius, with 24 seeds, the model cannot
     even establish the SIGN of the ZPF work.

34d. THE COST, QUANTIFIED.  sigma/|mean| = 5.32, so 10% precision on the

     five-point scan.  And the distribution is heavy-tailed -- one seed at
     4.6 sigma out of 24 -- so sigma/sqrt(N) UNDERSTATES what is needed.

34e. WHAT STANDS.  The radiated energy over the same 20 orbits is
     4.4017e-04 eV, matching the analytic Larmor value, so the loss side
     is right.  The crude four-seed means put |work|/radiation near 30 at
     a_pair and between 12 and 30 across the scanned radii -- but 34c says
     those means are not significant, so no balance radius is claimed and
     none should be quoted from this.

     Step 4 is therefore OPEN, with a correct method and a known price.
     It is the only route in the plan that can produce a Bohr scale, and
     it now needs a seed budget rather than a redesign.

35. PLAN STEPS 1 AND 2

35a. STEP 1 -- THE COMPTON BARRIER IS NO LONGER AN IMPORT.  It was entered
     as g*hbar/(4 m c) with a localization argument.  The same number is
     the electron's magnetic moment divided by charge times c:

       mu/(e c) = [(g/2)(e hbar/2m)]/(e c) = g hbar/(4 m c) = r*

     so r* is the only length that can be built from mu, e and c -- the
     length the moment configuration itself carries.  The localization
     reading is a second, independent argument landing on the same number,
     which is why it is now quoted as derived rather than chosen.

     Pinned in particle_species.hpp by

       static_assert(agreesTo(comptonBarrierRadius,
           magneticMoment(electron)/(elementaryCharge*speedOfLight),1e-15));

     BANDED, NOT EXACT, and the first attempt got this wrong: an exact
     static_assert failed.  The two sides associate the same
     multiplications differently and land 2.6e-16 apart, one or two ULPs.
     The band is still four orders tighter than the 1e-8 assert beside it.

     The expression itself is unchanged, so the constant stays bit-identical
     to every result measured with it.  Verified: the branch trace's
     default run reproduces t = 199.442442952 ps, a = 9.5507233815e-13 m,
     L = 0.0949953986509 hbar exactly.

35b. STEP 2 -- A CONTINUOUS STARTING RADIUS, AND WHAT IT SHOWS.

35c. THE CASCADE IS A CLOSED-FORM MAP WITH NO FIXED POINT.  Emitting
     hbar*omega = 2R/n^3 from a binding R/n^2 leaves R(n+2)/n^3, so

       n -> sqrt(n^3/(n+2)).

     Checked against all 20 measured transitions above: agreement 6e-7 to

     Where the pair came from leaves no trace; only where it is now

36. PLAN STEP 3: AN EMISSION RULE WITH MOMENTS IN IT -- PREDICTION
    FALSIFIED

    The default quantum hbar*omega_orb contains no moment, which is why
    section 35c's cascade map has no fixed point.  Step 3 replaces it with
    hbar*omega_spin -- one photon per spin precession -- so the moments
    enter the energy bookkeeping for the first time.  Added as
    CREM_PHOTON_FROM_SPIN, an option rather than a replacement, so nothing
    already measured is endangered; with it unset the branch trace is
    bit-identical (t = 199.442442952 ps, 3 photons).

36a. WHICH RATE, AND WHY NOT THE OBVIOUS ONE.  The mutual libration rate
     |omega1-omega2| is exactly zero for ortho by the exchange symmetry of
     section 27a, so it would switch emission off in one channel entirely.
     max(|omega1|,|omega2|) is defined in both and is what "per spin
     precession" means.  The orbit is resized to the energy each call is
     given, a = k/(2E) with |L| scaled as sqrt(a), so the quantum tracks
     the emission's own orbit as the classical branch does.

36b. THE DIRECTION WAS PREDICTED BEFORE MEASURING, AND HELD.
     omega_spin/omega_orb is 2.7e-5 at a_pair, so the quantum is four to
     five orders SMALLER and the photons become far more frequent, not
     rarer.  The plan's "one photon per 23 orbits" was a statement about
     the frequency ratio, not about the threshold an energy rule sets.
     Measured, from n=2:

       rule        collapse    photons    photon energy   stop
       default      199.4 ps         3    1.70 eV first   4.94 barriers
       spin        2188.4 ps    122245    8.63e-06 eV     14.55 barriers

36c. THE PREDICTION THAT MATTERED IS FALSE.  The plan said the cascade
     would acquire a distinguished radius at 0.726 lambda_C, the peak of
     omega_spin/omega_orb.  Photon density per unit ln(a), retardation
     limit relaxed to 2 so the run reaches 1.118 lambda_C -- within a
     factor 1.5 of the predicted peak:

       r/lambda_C    photons        r/lambda_C    photons
        960.3         40784          67.9            670
        736.8         25755          39.9            347
        565.3         17958          23.5            108
        433.7         12847          13.9             47
        332.8          9066           8.2             17
        255.3          4830           4.8              7

        115.3          1409           1.3              1

     Strictly monotone over three decades.  No peak anywhere, and none at
     0.726 lambda_C, where the density is one photon per bin and still
     falling.

36d. WHY THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG, and it was my own error in the plan.
     A peak in omega_spin/omega_orb is a peak in photons per ORBIT.  What
     marks a radius is photons per unit ln(a), which is the energy
     radiated per ln(a) divided by the quantum.  The quantum hbar*omega_s
     grows far faster than the available energy as the orbit tightens, so
     the density falls monotonically and the ratio's peak never reaches
     the observable.  Conflating the two is what made the prediction look
     falsifiable in the right way; it was falsifiable, and it is false.

36e. WHAT STANDS AFTER STEP 3.  Putting the moments into the emission
     rule does not give the model a level, a preferred radius, or any
     structure at all -- it only changes the graininess of a cascade that
     remains smooth and monotone.  Three routes in section 30-31 said the
     moments carry the Compton scale and cannot quantize; this is the
     fourth, and the first one that tested it dynamically rather than
     kinematically.

     The protected results are intact: validation 55/55, header isolation
     37/37, and the exchange-parity signature is unchanged (ortho
     |omega1-omega2| = 0 exactly, para 0.335074, both identical to
     section 27a).

37. PLAN STEP 5: THE ALPHA GAP, AND THE PLAN'S OWN FRAMING OF IT WAS WRONG

    The plan said: no rule built from mu, e, m and c can give the Bohr
    scale, because that needs alpha, and alpha in QED comes from the
    vertex.  Then two options -- declare alpha as the single import, or
    record a negative result.  Measured, the first half of that is false
    and the second half asks the wrong question.

37a. ALPHA IS NOT MISSING.  It is in the model already, as the coupling
     itself, and now named:

       alpha = k e^2/(hbar c) = 7.297352573749e-03,  1/alpha = 137.035999000
       k = alpha hbar c            agreement 3.9e-16 (ULP)

     And every length is the reduced Compton wavelength times a power of
     it, with nothing left over:

       a_pair / lambda_C = 274.0720     = 2/alpha        (ULP: 3.7e-16)
       r*     / lambda_C =   0.5006     = (g/2)/2        EXACT
       r_e    / lambda_C = 7.297353e-03 = alpha          EXACT
       R      = alpha^2 m c^2 / 4 = 6.802847 eV

     fineStructureConstant and reducedComptonWavelength are now derived
     constants in physical_constants.hpp, and particle_species.hpp pins all
     four relations with static_asserts.  qed_reference.hpp had been
     defining its own copy of the same alpha expression; that duplicate is
     gone, so there is one constant with four uses instead of two
     definitions.

     So the model has ONE dimensionful input, lambda_C -- which the moment
     configuration carries, since r* = mu/(e c) from section 35a -- and ONE
     dimensionless input, alpha.  Both are ordinary measured constants, and
     alpha is an input in QED too.  Nothing is missing on that side.

37b. WHAT IS ACTUALLY MISSING IS A REASON TO SIT AT a_pair.  The model
     computes the Bohr scale correctly and then has no mechanism that
     selects it.  Classically, at FIXED angular momentum,

       E(r) = L^2/(2 mu r^2) - k/r,  minimised at r = L^2/(mu k),

     and with L = hbar that is a_pair to 1.1e-13.  So the classical scale
     comes entirely from the assertion L = hbar -- import 1 in the README's
     table -- and not from alpha at all.

     That assertion is a Bohr-model argument, and quantum mechanics
     superseded it: the real 1S ground state has L = 0, so the scale cannot
     come from quantizing angular momentum.  In QM the same minimum comes
     from hbar^2/(2 mu r^2), the LOCALIZATION energy, which has the same
     algebra and a different origin.

37c. THE HONEST RESTATEMENT.  The model's scale content is not six imports
     and not one alpha.  It is exactly one assertion, L = hbar, standing in
     for a localization energy the model does not have.  The other five
     imports are rules about discreteness -- the emission quantum, the spin
     orientation, the photon's hbar -- and rules do not set a scale.  That
     is why steps 1 to 3 could remove the barrier from the import list,
     free the starting radius and put the moments into the emission rule
     without any level appearing anywhere: none of them touched the one
     assertion that carries the scale.

37d. AND IT IS WHY STEP 4 IS THE ONLY ROUTE LEFT.  A zero-point field
     supplies exactly what L = hbar is standing in for: a fluctuation that
     resists collapse without fixing the angular momentum.  That is the
     mechanism, not the scale -- the scale is already right.  Step 4's
     power balance is therefore not one option among several; after 37b it
     is the only thing in the model that could replace the assertion.

     Its price is known from section 34d: about 2825 field seeds per
     radius, and a heavy tail that makes that an underestimate.

38. STEP 4 AGAIN: ANTITHETIC PAIRING BUYS THE MEASUREMENT

    Section 34 left the method correct and the statistics hopeless -- 24
    seeds gave mean/error = 0.92 and a price of ~2825 seeds per radius.
    Paying that was the wrong move; the noise had structure to exploit.

38a. THE TRICK, AND WHY IT APPLIES EXACTLY HERE.  The field enters
     ZeroPointField::sample only through sin and cos of a phase carrying
     +phase[n], so adding pi to every phase gives exactly -E and -B.  The
     work then splits:

       W = W1 + W2 + O(F^4),
       W1 = integral of q E.v along the UNPERTURBED orbit  -- ODD in F,
       W2 = the absorption, where v itself responds to E   -- EVEN in F.

     W1 is the large random part that averages to zero only statistically;
     W2 is the physics.  Averaging a field with its own negation cancels W1
     PER PAIR rather than over an ensemble.

38b. MEASURED, r = a_pair, 20 orbits, 12 antithetic pairs (24 runs, the
     same cost as section 34c):

       estimator      mean (eV)     sigma (eV)    error       mean/error
       single field   2.7775e-04    2.0128e-02    5.8104e-03      0.05
       antithetic     3.3262e-03    5.0670e-03    1.4627e-03      2.27

     sigma falls 4.0x, so 16x fewer runs for the same precision, and the
     significance goes from nothing to 2.3 sigma on twelve pairs.  Eleven
     of the twelve pair values are positive.

38c. WHAT THAT SETTLES.  The SIGN is now established: the zero-point field
     PUMPS ENERGY IN, which is what the module always claimed and what
     section 33a's broken estimator had reported backwards.  And the size
     is large -- |work|/radiated = 7.63 at the Bohr radius over the same 20
     orbits, with the radiated 4.3620e-04 eV matching analytic Larmor.

     If that ratio stays above 1 at every radius, the field always wins and
     this model's SED has no bound state at all -- which is the known
     self-ionization problem of SED hydrogen, not a bug here.  The balance
     radius, if it exists, is where the ratio crosses 1.  That scan is
     running.

38d. THE REMAINING PRICE.  sigma/|mean| = 1.52 after pairing, so 10%
     precision needs about 232 pairs (464 runs) per radius rather than
     2825 seeds.  Two of twelve pairs still sit at four times the others,
     so the tail is thinner but not gone.

39. STEP 4 IS BLOCKED, AND THE BLOCKER IS THE ZPF'S ORBIT-TIED BAND

    Antithetic pairing (section 38) made the work measurable.  The first
    thing it made measurable is that the quantity being measured is not the
    one the balance needs.

39a. THE WORK GROWS AS t^2, NOT t.  r = a_pair, 10 antithetic pairs per
     point:

       orbits   work (eV)      error      mean/err   work/orbit   |W|/rad
         5      -3.3098e-04   2.50e-04     -1.32    -6.6195e-05    3.015
        10      +8.9244e-04   5.63e-04     +1.59    +8.9244e-05    4.069
        20      +3.7703e-03   1.72e-03     +2.19    +1.8851e-04    8.593

     work/orbit is not constant; it rises.  Two independent readings of the
     exponent agree:

       from the work,   10 -> 20 orbits:  4.22x  ->  p = 2.08
       from |W|/rad,    10 -> 20 orbits:  2.11x  ->  p = 2.08

     (The radiated energy is exactly linear in t, so |W|/rad scales as
     t^(p-1) and gives the exponent independently.)

     p = 1 is secular pumping, which is what an SED balance is made of.
     p = 2 is RESONANT absorption: a driven oscillator on exact resonance
     gains amplitude proportional to t and energy to t^2.

39b. WHAT THAT DOES TO THE RADIUS SCAN.  |W|/radiated is therefore not a
     physical quantity -- it depends on how long the run was.  At a_pair it
     is 3.0 after 5 orbits, 4.1 after 10 and 8.6 after 20, and would keep
     climbing.  The scan below is all at 20 orbits, so its rows are
     comparable with each other and none of them is a balance:

       a/a_pair   r/lambda_C   work (eV)     mean/err   |W|/rad at 20 orbits
       3           822.22      1.3540e-04      2.09        4.790
       1           274.07      3.3262e-03      2.27        7.626
       0.3          82.22      9.4805e-02      1.90       11.104
       0.1          27.41      1.7899e+00      2.02       14.832

     The trend is real and monotone -- the field wins harder the deeper the
     orbit -- but "|W|/rad = 1 is the balance radius" cannot be applied to
     it, and the crossing an extrapolation would put near 200 a_pair is not
     a result.  No balance radius is claimed.

39c. THE CAUSE IS STRUCTURAL AND WAS HALF-ANTICIPATED.  Section 32a
     checked that the orbit-tied band preserves the correct broadband
     AMPLITUDE law and concluded step 4 was alive.  That was right about
     the amplitude and wrong about the dynamics: because the band rides
     with the orbit, a mode at omega_orb stays at omega_orb forever, so the
     drive never decoheres.  A real broadband zero-point field has a finite
     correlation time -- the orbit drifts through the spectrum -- and gives
     absorption linear in t.  This one gives t^2 because it is permanently
     on resonance by construction.

39d. WHAT WOULD UNBLOCK IT.  A fixed broadband field instead of one tied
     to the orbital frequency.  The module's own comment says why the band
     was tied: its upper edge sets the integrator step, so a wide fixed

45. PAIRING ACROSS RADII: THE VARIANCE CUT SECTION 44d ASKED FOR

     on the SAME seed and taking the per-seed difference of the
     Larmor-normalized works cancels that shared part, which is most of the
     variance.

47. LOCKING N5, AND IT TURNS OUT TO BE AN EXACT IDENTITY

    N5 was recorded as "the collapse time scales as alpha^-5 lambda_C/c,
    the same power as Gamma_2gamma".  Written down properly it is not a
    scaling but an equality.

47a. THE ALGEBRA.  The dipole inspiral coefficient is

       C = 2 q^2 k /(6 pi eps0 c^3 mu^2),

     which for e+e- (q = e, mu = m/2, k = alpha hbar c) reduces to

       3 C = 16 alpha^2 lambda_C^2 c,

     so the closed-form time from a_pair = 2 lambda_C/alpha to zero is

       t = a_pair^3/(3C) = lambda_C/(2 alpha^5 c) = (1/2) alpha^-5 lambda_C/c

     against the QED lifetime tau_2gamma = 2 hbar/(alpha^5 m c^2)
     = 2 alpha^-5 lambda_C/c.  Measured: t/unit = 0.500000000 to nine
     digits, t = 31.1235 ps, tau_2gamma = 124.4942 ps, ratio exactly 4.

47b. WHAT IS AND IS NOT THE CONTENT.  alpha enters this model in exactly
     one place, the coupling k = alpha hbar c.  The exponent 5 then comes
     out of a^3/C with a ~ 1/alpha and C ~ alpha^2 -- three powers from the
     radius and two from the coefficient.  Nothing put a fifth power
     anywhere.  That is the non-tautological part.

     The factor 4 between the two times is NOT predicted by anything here
     and is not claimed to be.  It is the whole gap between a classical
     inspiral time and an annihilation rate, and sections 24 to 26 are
     about why.

47c. LOCKED as inspiral-alpha-power, asserting t/(alpha^-5 lambda_C/c) =
     1/2 to 1e-12 for the positronium pair and finiteness for the others,
     which carry their own reduced mass and dipole charge.  Verified to
     fail: moving the target to 0.6 gives FAILED: inspiral-alpha-power at
     56/57.

     Locked because the emission rule, the starting radius and the
     preparation have all been changed in this codebase without anyone
     checking that this identity still held.

48. LOCKING N6: THE ONE HYPERFINE TERM THE MODEL HAS CONTRIBUTES NOTHING

    N6 was recorded as "the classical dipole-dipole energy as a fraction of
    the hyperfine splitting, with the remainder correctly identified".  The
    lockable part is the boundary, not the fraction.

48a. THE STATEMENT.  The measured o-Ps/p-Ps splitting is
     (7/12) alpha^4 m c^2 = 204.4 GHz and divides into a Fermi contact term
     (4/7) and virtual annihilation (3/7).  Neither exists in this model.
     What does exist is the tensor coupling

       U = (mu0/4 pi r^3) [mu1.mu2 - 3 (mu1.nhat)(mu2.nhat)],

51. IS MAGNETIC DIPOLE REPULSION THE REASON TO SIT AT a_pair?

    Proposed as the replacement for L = hbar: the pair is held not by a
    quantized angular momentum but by the repulsion between its magnetic
    moments.  The shape is right -- a repulsive r^-3 term outgrows the r^-1
    Coulomb attraction at short range, so the two MUST produce a minimum
    with L = 0 and no quantization.  The question is where.

51a. WHERE THE MINIMUM IS, EXACTLY.  For point dipoles aligned and
     perpendicular to the separation, U = +C/r^3 with C = (mu0/4pi) mu^2, and
     V(r) = -k/r + C/r^3 has dV/dr = 0 at r_min = sqrt(3C/k).  The ratio C/k
     is an identity:

       C/k = (mu/(e c))^2 = r*^2        measured ratio 1.000000000000

     so the minimum is

       r_min = sqrt(3) r* = 3.348e-13 m = 0.867 lambda_C = a_pair / 316.1
       depth  V(r_min) = -(2/3) k/r_min = -2.867 keV   (Bohr n=1: -6.80 eV)

     The mechanism is real, exact and needs no L = hbar -- and it puts the
     pair at the Compton scale, 316 times inside the Bohr radius, bound 420
     times more deeply.  At a_pair itself the ideal force ratio is 1.0e-5.

51b. WHY IT CANNOT LAND ANYWHERE ELSE.  C/k = r*^2 contains mu, e and c and
     nothing else, so any balance of dipole repulsion against Coulomb sits
     at r* times a pure number.  a_pair = 2 lambda_C/alpha is r* times
     4/(alpha g/2): one power of alpha away, the same gap as sections 30-31.
     This is the sixth independent route to the Compton scale.

51c. THE MODEL'S OWN FORCE AGREES, AND ADDS TWO OBSTACLES.  Full covariant
     dipole force against Coulomb, circular orbit, moments along the orbit
     normal (tools/para_ortho_dipole_force):

       R/a_pair   para F_radial   para |F_d/F_C|   ortho F_radial   ortho |F_d/F_C|
       1          attractive        1.66e-05        attractive        3.67e-05
       0.2        attractive        4.78e-05        attractive        5.48e-04
       0.05       REPULSIVE         1.62e-03        attractive        6.39e-03
       0.005      REPULSIVE         3.26e-01        attractive        4.76e-01
       0.00183    REPULSIVE         5.23e-01        attractive        5.28e-01

     (1) At the Bohr radius the para dipole force is not repulsive at all;
     it turns repulsive only between 0.2 and 0.05 a_pair.
     (2) Ortho is ATTRACTIVE at every radius.  Anti-aligned moments have no
     barrier and no minimum, so the mechanism could hold at most one of the
     two channels and cannot be what sets a common scale.

     Against the ideal-dipole ratio 3 r*^2/r^2 the model tracks well down to
     0.005 a_pair (0.33 against 0.40) and then falls short at the barrier
     (0.52 against 2.99), so inside the model the balance point is not
     reached above r*.  Which correction does that was not isolated; the
     short-range regulator is not it, since at 193 fm against its 83.6 fm
     radius and exponent 12 it is within a part in 10^4 of unity.

51d. AND THE ORIENTATION AVERAGE REMOVES IT ENTIRELY.  U is repulsive only
     for moments perpendicular to the separation; along it, U = -2C/r^3 and
     attracts.  Its average over directions is exactly zero -- the identity
     already locked as dipole-tensor-s-state-average (section 48).  So for
     any state not pinned to one orientation the repulsion supplies no
     radial barrier at all.

51e. VERDICT.  The dipole repulsion is a genuine stationarity mechanism that
     replaces L = hbar, and it is worth recording as such.  It does not
     explain a_pair: it balances Coulomb at sqrt(3) mu/(e c), the Compton
     scale; at a_pair it is 1e-5 of Coulomb and of the wrong sign; it
     attracts in the ortho channel; and it averages to zero over
     orientations.

52. THE 0.52-AGAINST-2.99 DISCREPANCY AT THE BARRIER, RESOLVED -- AND IT
    CORRECTS SECTION 51

    Section 51c left open why the model's dipole force at the barrier is
    0.52 of Coulomb against 2.99 for ideal dipoles.  The suspicion raised
    was that not all magnetic fields are included.  They are; the gap is
    three other things, and one of them hides a real result.

52a. NOTHING IS MISSING.  The force on the target is grad(mu_lab.B +
     p_lab.E), with the partner's field = Lienard-Wiechert charge field
     (E and B) + retarded magnetic dipole field (E and B).  Rebuilding it
     from those exact fields, channel by channel, reproduces the model's
     own force to four digits wherever no regularization acts:

       r/r*   model F      sum of exact channels
       3.0    1.841e-04    1.841e-04
       2.5    3.901e-04    3.900e-04
       2.2    6.596e-04    6.593e-04
       2.0    9.751e-04    9.747e-04

52b. CAUSE 1 -- MY "IDEAL" OMITTED TWO REAL CHANNELS.  The partner's CHARGE
     field acts on the moving target twice: its magnetic field on mu, and
     its electric field on the induced electric dipole p = gamma v x mu/c^2.
     Both are attractive and equal to four digits (-2.252e-05 and
     -2.252e-05 at 3 r*), together -19.7% of the dipole repulsion at 3 r*
     and -15% at 1.6 r*.  This is genuine physics, of relative size
     ~ beta r/r*, and it is what turns para's dipole force attractive above
     ~0.1 a_pair.  The dipole's own electric field on p is +0.2%.

52c. CAUSE 2 -- THE DIPOLE FIELD IS REGULARIZED, DIFFERENTLY BY BRANCH.
     separationFloor() returns comptonBarrierRadius for e+e-, and
     clampedSeparationVector replaces r by sqrt(r^2 + r*^2).

       static source   Plummer-softened at EVERY radius, on every path
                       including the one named "exact":
                       B/B_ideal = q^3/(q^2+1)^(3/2), matched to 5e-6 at
                       q = 0.8..8; force = q^5/(q^2+1)^(5/2), matched to
                       2.5e-4 (0.9212 at 5.5 r*, 0.1776 at r*)
       moving source   exact point dipole above 2 r* (0.9995 of ideal at
                       1.75 r*), smoothstep blend towards the softened field
                       between r* and 2 r*, softened below r*
       charge field    exact above r* (1.00000), FROZEN at its r* value
                       below (E at 0.8 r* equals E at r* exactly) -- a hard
                       clamp, not the Plummer form

     The code states the reason for the floor and it is NUMERICAL: a kink
     in the force at the old hard clamp stalled the adaptive integrator's
     step-doubling probe.  The Plummer form removed the kink.  Three fields
     now carry three different short-range treatments.

52d. CAUSE 3 -- AT AND BELOW r* EVERYTHING IS FLOORED.  The "exact" dipole
     field itself returns 0.34 of ideal at r = r*, and the charge-field
     channels collapse there (-1.5e-05 against -7.5e-04 at 1.1 r*).

52e. THE RESULT HIDDEN BY THE REGULARIZATION.  With exact fields on the
     model's circular orbit, the dipole repulsion net of the motional
     attraction DOES overtake Coulomb:

       r/r*    exact F / F_Coulomb
       1.750      0.8358
       1.700      0.8880
       1.660      0.9334
       1.630      0.9696
       1.610      0.9949
       1.600      1.0080
       1.550      1.0771
       1.500      1.1533

     Crossing at r = 1.606 r* = 310 fm.  So classical electrodynamics with
     point moments has a stationary point for the para geometry, needing no
     L = hbar -- and it sits exactly inside the r*..2r* zone the model
     regularizes, which is why the model's ratio peaks near 0.5 and never
     reaches 1.  A numerical fix for an integrator kink erases the one
     classical stationary point the moments produce.

     Caveat on the number: the circular speed assumes Coulomb alone
     supplies the centripetal force, which stops being true as the ratio
     approaches 1.  The moving crossing 1.606 r* and the static ideal
     crossing sqrt(3) r* = 1.732 r* therefore bracket the true stationary
     radius rather than fix it.

53. DYNAMIC TEST, STEP 1: THE FLOOR SWITCH, AND THE BARRIER HOLDS PARA

    Section 52 found a classical stationary point for para at 1.6-1.7 r*,
    hidden because separationFloor() regularizes the r*..2r* zone.  A force
    balance on a prescribed circular orbit is not a trajectory, so this
    tests it with real integration.

53a. THE SWITCH.  CREM_SEPARATION_FLOOR_SCALE multiplies separationFloor(),
     read once per process; unset, the function returns
     comptonBarrierRadius itself.  Checked: header isolation 37/37,

     (t = 199.442442952 ps, L = 0.0949953986509 hbar).  The stopping rule
     reads comptonBarrierRadius directly and does not move.

     DISABLED so the outcome reflects forces and the integrator only.  Para
     = moments aligned along z, perpendicular to the x separation (the
     repulsive geometry); ortho = anti-aligned.  Released at r0 with a
     tangential speed that is a fraction of the circular speed.

53c. AN ENERGY CHECK THAT IS NOT ONE.  Frame boundFieldEnergy is the
     "exact discrete world-tube balance" reservoir -- bound near field,
     retardation, interference AND discretization remainder -- assigned as
     balanceStart - dE_mech - dE_rad.  E_mech + E_rad + boundField is
     therefore conserved BY CONSTRUCTION and says nothing.  The independent
     quantity is the mechanical energy, and every energy statement below
     uses it.

     Start 3 r*, v_t = 0.2 v_circ:

54. DYNAMIC TEST, STEP 3: PRECESSING MOMENTS -- THE BARRIER IS NOT A

    perpendicular to the separation.  Section 48's lock says the orientation

55. THE ACCURACY DEFECT AT THE BOTTOM OF THE ORBIT: TWO NODE CROSSINGS

    Section 54 was blocked by the documented defect: near the barrier the
    step-doubling error stops shrinking with the step, so CREM_MAX_DEPTH is
    a treadmill.  Reproduced on the tilted para case (floor 0.25 r*, tilt 30
    deg, NumericalFailure at 1.1189 r*) by copying the engine one outer step
    before the failure and sweeping the evaluation time across the failing
    step.  Both causes below are the same fact about the history:
    interpolatedCharge() is a cubic Hermite through position and velocity,
    so it is C1 -- the source acceleration JUMPS at every stored node.  On
    this orbit the jump is 1-6% of |a| at all 119 nodes, and the stored node
    accelerations sit halfway between the left and right limits (checked
    against a second-order finite difference of the stored velocities: the
    residual matches the truncation estimate at every node).  Any force whose
    retarded time crosses a node therefore steps, and step doubling sees a
    step as an error of order dt^0-dt^1.

55a. CAUSE 1: THE TWO POLES OF THE POINT DIPOLE.  The near-field dipole is
     built as the limit of two charges 1e-5 r apart with charge ~1/separation.
     Each pole solved its own retarded time, so within separation/c of a node
     the poles read accelerations from different segments and the jump was
     multiplied by the pole charge: the retarded time landed 5.9e-30 s from
     node 106 (jump 0.35%) and the dipole field was wrong by up to a factor 9
     in a window whose width scaled with the pole separation.  FIX: both poles
     are expanded from ONE segment -- the one holding the central retarded
     time -- as (x, v, a, j) Taylor series, which reproduce the cubic exactly
     inside it and continue it smoothly past its ends
     (historicalChargeWithJerk).  Bad windows at pole separations 1e-5, 1e-6,
     1e-7: 0; the dipole-field jump fell 3500x (6.3e-1 -> 1.8e-4).  The
     far-zone two-charge construction is NOT changed: its poles' emission
     times differ by ~r/c, so they legitimately sit on different segments.
     The trajectory still failed at the same place.

56. THE THIRD DISCONTINUITY (55f): A FLOOR CLAMP APPLIED PER POLE

    Section 55f left the tilted para 30 deg run failing at 0.33 r* (floor
    0.25 r*), with a step-doubling metric that fell irregularly (29x, then
    11870x) and a guess that the charge-charge Lienard-Wiechert force was
    the unpinned culprit.  The failing substep was captured from inside the
    engine (start state, history, dt = 7.95e-27 s; a temporary hook, since
    removed) and every force component was evaluated on a 400-point grid

56a. THE GUESS WAS WRONG.  Second differences over the grid, normalized by
     the force, against their own median:

       component                          max / median
       Lienard-Wiechert, both particles       1.5
       dipole-field Lorentz force            3.1-3.3
       gradient force on the positron         4.5
       gradient force on the electron     2.6e6   at 0.732 dt

     The electron's gradient force is 56x its Coulomb force here.  Its
     spatial part is smooth; the jump is in the material term, whose rate
     went -1.1884e8 -> -1.2055e8 (1.4%) between evaluation times 4e-31 s
     apart.  Of the rate's three time samples only the one a
     derivativeStep back moved (by 2.85e-20, the other two by ~5e-23), and
     only in its dipole channel.  Two further guesses were measured and
     refuted: all three samples sit in the same history segment, 1.7e-23 s
     from the nearest node, so no node crossing (a segment pin written for
     it was removed again); and the moment-derivative stencil sits 5.6e-24 s
     from any node, with the moment and both derivatives smooth to 1e-9.

56b. THE CAUSE.  In twoChargeLimitDipoleField each pole's field used
     max(distance, nuclearCutoff, separationFloor()) of ITS OWN retarded
     distance.  At the failing sample the dipole's retarded distance was
     4.832589933e-14 m, 1.46e-20 m above the floor, and the two pole
     distances differ by 4.1e-20 m: one pole was clamped, the other was
     not.  Their difference -- the dipole field -- then carried the
     unbalanced 1/d^2 of one pole, amplified by 1/poleSeparationFraction:

       pole separation fraction      1e-5          1e-6          1e-7
       U across 4e-31 s          -1.61649e-14  -1.567469e-14  -1.567468e-14
                              -> -1.61617e-14  (flat)        (flat, noise)

     A linear ramp of 2e-4 within 4e-31 s, i.e. 5e26 1/s against the
     ~5e21 1/s the physics allows, and a value 3% off the limit the finer
     separations agree on.  The estimated slope of one unbalanced pole,
     |pole| * 2v/d = 9e26 1/s, matches.  The blend in
     retardedMagneticDipoleField weights the exact field low here, so the
     force saw 2.85e-20 of it -- enough to fail at maximum depth.

56c. FIX.  Both poles are regularized by one factor set by the dipole's own
     retarded distance d_c: fieldDistance = distance * max(1,
     max(nuclearCutoff, floor)/d_c).  Above the floor no pole is clamped;
     below it both are scaled alike; at the floor the factor is exactly 1,
     so the field is continuous.  On the captured state the 1e-5 field is
     now -1.5674687e-14, flat to 3e-7 over the window, and agrees with 1e-6
     and 1e-7.  The far-zone construction has no per-pole clamp and is
     unchanged.

74. STEP 4, MEASUREMENT 1: FREE EVOLUTION UNDER THE ZPF, FIRST ROUND

    Sections 42-50 measured the power ratio at fixed radii and found no
    crossing.  This asks the direct question instead: started at different
    radii and left alone under the zero-point field and radiation reaction,
    do orbits drift toward one radius?

74a. WHY NOT THE COLLAPSE ESTIMATOR.  estimateCremCollapse extrapolates the
     secular energy loss as deltaEnergyPerOrbit = -orbitalRadiatedEnergy/mu:
     the radiated flux only.  The ZPF's work never enters the secular
     evolution, so the estimator cannot show a ZPF balance at all.  The
     measurement has to integrate the orbit directly.

74b. SETUP.  Circular orbit, moments along the normal, default model (unified
     Plummer, floor 0.05 r*), --radiation-reaction individual (continuous
     Landau-Lifshitz, no photons, no ladder), ZPF scale 1, band [0.3, 3] x
     omega_orb, 64 modes.  Antithetic pairs: the same field realization with
     every phase shifted by pi, so the pair mean cancels the drift that is
     linear in the field.  Radius measured by the energy, a = -k/(2 E_mech),
     because the instantaneous separation of a non-circular orbit is
     misleading (at 0.01 a_pair with the field off it rose by 14% while the
     energy fell).  6 seeds x 2 phases per radius.

74c. RESULT.  ln(a/a0) at mid-window and end:

       start       orbits  field  pairs expanding  ln a at 50%      ln a at 100%
       0.01 a_pair   40     off         -          -0.049           -0.095
       0.01 a_pair   40     on        5 of 5      +0.67 +- 0.17     +2.3 +- 0.8
       0.03 a_pair  200     off         -          -0.077           -0.178
       0.03 a_pair  200     on        5 of 6      +1.81 +- 0.80     +2.6 +- 1.0

     (unbound states counted as ln a = 10; one run at 0.01 a_pair failed
     numerically after passing through an unbound state, its pair dropped.)
     Without the field the orbit shrinks, as it must.  With it, deep orbits
     are driven OUTWARD fast: 7 of 12 runs at 0.01 a_pair and 2 of 12 at

     the mid-window expansion is 4 sigma; 0.03 a_pair expands from the
     start in 10 of 12 runs.

     at that radius the drive is fluctuation-dominated, orbits wander by
     factors of 2-4, and a quarter of the runs ionize within 2000 orbits.
     That is the known SED picture (a balance only in the mean, with
     self-ionization through large excursions), not a ground state.  Orbits
     that start at the Bohr radius shrink at the radiative rate toward that
     region; following them there takes ~radiative-collapse times (6e5
     orbits), which was not attempted.

75f. LIMITS.  4 pairs and one control per radius; the orbit-tied band
     [0.3,3] x omega_orb and 64 modes throughout (a fixed broadband field
     was not tested); no start between 0.1 and 0.3 a_pair, so the crossing
     is bracketed, not located; the ionizations at 0.1 a_pair were not
     checked against the energy bookkeeping.

77. APSIDAL PRECESSION ADDED TO THE SECULAR LAYER

77a. WHAT WAS ADDED.  orbitAveragedApsidalRate (secular_spin_orbit.hpp):
     the secular Gauss equation for the Runge-Lenz vector, averaged in time
     over the Kepler ellipse, projected on the in-plane direction normal to
     e and divided by |e|.  advanceCoupledSecularSpinOrbit evaluates it once
     per substep at the midpoint state and rotates the apsidal line about
     the new normal.  e and the plane stay governed by |L| and J as before.
     CREM_NO_APSIDAL_PRECESSION restores the old behaviour.  Below e=1e-4
     the rate is set to zero.

80. THE 4/3 SPIN-ORBIT FACTOR: MISSING THOMAS BACK-REACTION IN THE
    INSTANTANEOUS FORCE SUM

80a. THE FINDING OF 77b.  The instantaneous pair pairDipoleForce +
     chargeDipoleForces had the same dipole-dipole sector as the retarded
     assembly but a spin-orbit sector 4/3 as large (apsidal rate 0.01105
     against 0.00829 per particle at a_pair).

80b. WHICH MODEL IS RIGHT.  Probe soprobe.cpp, orbit averages over Kepler
     nodes (e=0.3; a_pair and 0.1 a_pair), moment on one particle only:
       - third law: the instantaneous sum cancels exactly, the retarded one
         to 1e-5..1e-4 (field momentum);
       - orbit-averaged work: zero in both;
       - angular momentum: torque on the orbit against -dS/dt from the
         orbit-averaged Thomas-BMT rates.  Retarded: residual 3.2e-4 and
         3.8e-4.  Instantaneous: residual 0.333, torque = 4/3 dS/dt.
     So the retarded assembly conserves J = L + S with BMT spins, and the
     instantaneous one does not.

80c. WHY 4/3.  BMT contains Thomas precession, omega_T = -(v x a)/(2c^2).
     The instantaneous force comes from L = q (v_charge - v_dipole).A_mu,
     which couples the moment only to its rest-frame magnetic field and has
     no counterpart of omega_T.  For spin p in the Coulomb field of o the
     orbital coupling weight is g_p/(2 m_p m_o) + (g_p-1)/(2 m_p^2) with
     Thomas and g_p/(2 m_p m_o) + g_p/(2 m_p^2) without it (Barker and
     O'Connell): 3/2 against 2 for equal masses, 1/2 against 1 for
     m_o >> m_p.  The predicted ratios are 4/3 and 2.

80d. TEST OF THE EXPLANATION.  Thomas Lagrangian

82g. LIMITS.  One eccentricity and tilt in the flow checks; the retarded
     flow uses a causal history built at each state, not an engine history;
     crem_trajectory's frame and Schott diagnostics still call the ledger
     with weight 1 whatever the force model; the 1e-6-level differences of
     the two forms on symmetric states were not resolved.

83. ATTRACTION OF THE MOMENTS IN p-Ps, REPULSION IN o-Ps: WHAT THE MODEL
    HAS, AND A SIGN REGRESSION IN ITS CONTACT TERM

83a. QUESTION.  Is an effect missing that attracts the magnetic moments in
     para and repels them in ortho -- perhaps orbit-orbit?

83b. ORBIT-ORBIT.  The Breit orbit-orbit (current-current) interaction is
     the Darwin term, present as darwinForces/darwinInteractionEnergy.  It
     does not depend on spin and cannot separate para from ortho.

83c. THE TENSOR TERM IS PRESENT; ITS SIGN FOLLOWS THE TILT.  Azimuth average
     in the orbit plane, parallel moments (para), units mu0 mu^2/(4 pi r^3),
     positive = repulsive (probe ddsign.cpp):

     r/r*    tilt 0    30       54.7     60       90
     10      +1.000    +0.625   0.000    -0.125   -0.500
     1       +0.996    +0.624   +0.002   -0.122   -0.494
     0.2     +0.913    +0.591   +0.054   -0.054   -0.376

     Ortho has the opposite sign.  Moments along the orbit normal: para
     repels, ortho attracts; beyond the magic angle the reverse.  Averaged
     over orientations it vanishes (N6, dipole-tensor-s-state-average), and
     production samples the tilt isotropically, so the ensemble carries no
     net para attraction from it.

83d. THE EFFECT ASKED ABOUT IS THE FERMI CONTACT TERM, AND ITS SIGN IS NOW
     WRONG.  In QED the attraction of p-Ps and repulsion of o-Ps is the
     contact part of the spin-spin coupling, -(2 mu0/3) mu1.mu2 delta(r)
     (4/7 of the 204.4 GHz splitting; the other 3/7, virtual annihilation,
     acts in ortho only and has no classical counterpart here).  A current
     loop's field carries it: integral B dV = +(2 mu0/3) m.  cb607c9
     (2026-08-29) measured exactly that for the then-regularized field
     (7.77694e-30 against 7.77839e-30).
     The Plummer unification 00f0071 (2026-09-14, section 66) replaced the
     field by (mu0/4 pi)[3 r(r.m) - m rho^2]/rho^5, which is the softened
     field of a MAGNETIC-CHARGE dipole: integral B dV = -0.336 mu0 m
     (expected -1/3), i.e. the contact term with the opposite sign and half
     the strength.  Since then contact makes para repel and ortho attract,
     the reverse of QED.  Isotropic average of U at fixed r, para, in the
     units above: +2.58e-3 at 1 r*, +5.38e-2 at 0.2 r*, +1.77e-1 at
     0.05 r* (repulsive).  It is also inconsistent inside the model: the
     charge-dipole force uses the loop vector potential m x r/rho^3 but
     this field for B.

83e. THE CORRECT PLUMMER FIELD (probe loopfield.cpp).  The curl of the
     same vector potential,
         B = (mu0/4 pi) [3 r(r.m) - m (r^2 - 2 eps^2)] / rho^5
           = current form + (mu0/4 pi) 3 eps^2 m / rho^5,
     checked: B = curl A to 3.4e-10, div B = 0, integral B dV = +0.653 mu0
     (+2/3 up to the finite sphere), far field equal to the current form
     within 3 eps^2/r^2 (4.6e-3 at 1 r*).  Isotropic average for para turns
     attractive: -4.87e-3 at 1 r*, -1.07e-1 at 0.2 r*, -3.54e-1 at 0.05 r*.
     Energy U = -mu2.B1 = current U - (mu0/4 pi) 3 eps^2 (mu1.mu2)/rho^5.

83f. RELEVANCE TO PRODUCTION: NONE MEASURABLE.  Trace 42/2 (CREM_SKIP
     census, 63 checkpoints each): the smallest checkpoint periapsis is
     39.7 r* (para) and 40.8 r* (ortho); only the final photon cascade takes
     the circular orbit to ~4.9 r*.  The contact correction relative to the
     tensor term, 3 (eps/r_p)^2, is 5e-6 at 40 r* and 3e-4 at 5 r*.  Even
     with the right sign, the attraction the question has in mind is an
     S-state property (|psi(0)|^2), and the model's L = hbar orbits never
     sample r = 0 (cb607c9).

83g. WHAT A FIX WOULD TOUCH (not done here).  pairDipoleField,
     pairDipoleInteractionEnergy and pairDipoleForce (Plummer branch), the
     contact correction in the retarded two-charge dipole construction
     (retardedMagneticDipoleField and the far-zone field), the Plummer
     branch of azimuthAveragedDipoleEnergy, and the validation references
     moved in section 66.  Expected effect: nil in production (83f); real
     for deep or radial orbits, floor-scale experiments and the sign of the
     para/ortho contact physics the model claims to represent.

85. CONTACT HYPERFINE ENERGY OF A MICROCANONICAL ORBIT ENSEMBLE (STEP 1)

85a. IDEA.  The contact term (section 84) needs a state with density at
     r = 0.  A single radial orbit was rejected (cb607c9): its density
     diverges as r^-3/2 and overshoots the splitting by ~190x.  A classical
     ensemble at the 1S energy with ALL angular momenta (microcanonical,
     isotropic in phase space) weights small L by L dL, which softens the
     divergence to r^-1/2 and the regulator dependence to eps^-1/2.

85b. METHOD (probe contactensemble.cpp).  Only the dipole-dipole energy
     depends on position alone; velocity-linear terms average to zero in an
     isotropic ensemble.  Exact integral, no sampling: radial density
     rho(r) = sqrt(1/r - 1/2a) / [(pi^2/4)(2a)^(5/2)] on r < 2a (normalized to
     1.00000), log radial grid, 194-point sphere rule, with the model's own
     pairDipoleInteractionEnergy (current-loop Plummer field), moments
     parallel (para) or antiparallel (ortho).  The smearing scale is the
     separation floor, set by CREM_SEPARATION_FLOOR_SCALE.  Control: the same
     integral over the quantum 1S density |psi|^2.  Reference: the QED
     contact part of the splitting, 4/7 of (7/12) alpha^4 m c^2
     = 4.8301e-4 eV; its classical-spin analogue at |psi(0)|^2,
     2 (2 mu0/3) mu^2 |psi(0)|^2 = 2.4207e-4 eV (classical moments give
     +-1 where sigma1.sigma2 gives +1 and -3: a factor 2).

85c. RESULT.

     eps        <U>_para      <U>_ortho     dE           <n>/|psi0|^2   dE/QED contact
     0.05 r*    -2.6425e-3    +2.6425e-3    5.2849e-3    21.83          10.94
     r*         -5.9052e-4    +5.9052e-4    1.1810e-3     4.879          2.445
     2 r*       -4.1730e-4    +4.1730e-4    8.3460e-4     3.448          1.728
     (energies in eV)

     Quantum 1S control with the same field: <n>/|psi0|^2 = 0.9993, 0.9926,
     0.9857 and dE = 2.419e-4, 2.403e-4, 2.386e-4 eV, i.e. the classical-spin
     analogue to 0.1-1.4%: the integral and the field are right.
     - Para is bound deeper and ortho less, as in QED.
     - Scaling is eps^-1/2 to four digits: 21.83/4.879 = 4.475 against
       sqrt(20) = 4.472; 4.879/3.448 = 1.415 against sqrt(2) = 1.414.

       2 (mu0/4 pi) 3 eps^2 mu^2 / a^5 ~ 1e-9 eV at eps = r*.

85d. WHAT IT DOES NOT SHOW.  The microcanonical L distribution is assumed,
     not produced by the dynamics (that is step 3, the ZPF); the density
     still diverges as r^-1/2, so the answer depends on eps, only weakly;
     the choice of eps as a physical size of the moment (step 2) is not yet
     argued beyond N1; virtual annihilation (3/7 of the splitting) stays
     outside the model.
