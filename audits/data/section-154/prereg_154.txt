THE LIMIT BEING CLOSED.  Section 153 measured prepared states only and
said so.  Audit 113 measured that the dynamics tilts the terminal
measure.  So the identity of 153c -- <U> = 0 because
<(mu1.n)(mu2.n)> = <mu1.mu2>/3 for a pair isotropic about the orbital
normal, with b = -3a -- is a statement about the preparation and not yet
about what annihilates.

WHY I EXPECT IT TO BREAK, AND HOW.  The spin transport precesses each
moment about axes set by the orbital normal and the partner's field.
Precession about n CONSERVES each (mu_i.n) and moves only the azimuth, so
the tensor structure <(mu1.n)(mu2.n)> should be nearly frozen while the
isotropic one <mu1.mu2> moves -- which is exactly the quantity 113 saw
compress.  The two then stop being in the ratio 1/3 and the cancellation
fails.

THE MEASUREMENT.  The production path -- continuous Landau-Lifshitz,
photons off, which is the default since audit 109 -- one trajectory per
seed per conditioned channel, the terminal moments and the terminal
orbital vector read from the last CREM_DEBUG_SPINPAIR line of each.  Then
the same six averages 153 computed, at annihilation instead of at
preparation.

RULES, decided now.
R154a  THE IDENTITY SURVIVES if the terminal <(mu1.n)(mu2.n)>/<mu1.mu2>
       stays within 10% of 1/3 in both channels.
R154b  IF IT BREAKS, report which structure moved: the prediction above is
       that the tensor one is frozen and the isotropic one moves, and the
       opposite result refutes the mechanism rather than the finding.
R154c  Report <|m|^2/mu^2> at annihilation per channel and the M1 power
       ratio, against 153's prepared 3.4998, 1.5187 and 2.3045.
R154d  Report the completion rate; censored trajectories are excluded from

R154e  Audit 113's finding -- the dynamics tilts the terminal measure --

PREDICTION (mine).  R154a fails: the tensor structure holds to a few
percent while the isotropic one moves by tens of percent, so <U> becomes
nonzero and of the order of a few tenths of the 4.1 GHz component scale.
The M1 ratio moves toward 1, because the terminal angles compress toward
the middle of the band.
