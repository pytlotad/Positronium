WHAT 135b SAID AND WHY IT MAY BE WRONG.  Section 135b reported that under
the production reaction model the M1 channel "books nothing", from the
CREM_M1_SHARE line reading zero.  Reading further: that line prints the
SECULAR analytic decomposition, which is gated on isStochastic -- and so
is its E1 counterpart, which also prints zero although production plainly
radiates.  Meanwhile the ENGINE carries dipoleRadiationReaction, whose
torques applyDipoleRadiationTorque applies under every reaction model
except `disabled`, and in the continuous path the orbit's energy loss is
MEASURED from the integrated orbit rather than taken from the secular
formula.  So the zero may be a diagnostic gap and not a physical one, and
135b would then be wrong in exactly the way that matters.

THE THREE THINGS TO SETTLE.
  T1  Is the M1 reaction torque live in production, i.e. does removing it
      change a production trajectory?
  T2  Is the channel CLOSED in the engine: does the torque remove the
      energy the flux carries away?
  T3  What does production omit once T1 and T2 are answered, and how big
      is it?

RULES, decided now.
R136a  T1 is answered YES if a production trajectory with the M1 torque
       suppressed differs from the same trajectory with it active, in any
       printed digit of the terminal moments or the collapse time.  If it
       is bit-identical, 135b stands and the torque is inert in production.
R136b  T2, the closure, is answered on a controlled configuration rather
       than on a trajectory, because M1/E1 is of order 1e-15 there and the
       difference would be below double precision.  The channel is closed
       if the torque's work rate, averaged over a full precession cycle,
       matches the flux power within a factor 2.  Sign matters: the torque
       must REMOVE energy, and a match with the wrong sign is a failure.
R136c  The diagnostic is repaired if CREM_M1_SHARE prints the true E1 and
       M1 powers under the production model too, AND the trajectory stays
       bit-identical: the repair is diagnostics-only, so any change in the
       collapse time refutes it and must be reverted rather than explained.
R136d  Report M1/E1 along a production collapse, so "production accounts
       for all physical effects" carries a number and not an adjective.

PREDICTION (mine).  T1 yes, the torque is live -- so 135b's sentence about
production is wrong and the section says so in those words.  T2 closed to
within the Schott term, i.e. matching once averaged over a cycle and not
instantaneously.  T3 the remaining omission is not the channel but its
SECULAR bookkeeping: between checkpoints the estimator skips orbits using
a loss that carries no M1 term, so the M1 sink acts only during the
measured orbits, and the size of that is the M1/E1 ratio of R136d, which
I expect near 1e-15 at a_pair.
