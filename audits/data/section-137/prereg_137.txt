THE REQUEST.  Carry out the repair 136g named: drop the magnetic term
from expectedLossPerOrbit, on the reasoning that the engine's M1 torque
already pays for that radiation.

WHAT I FOUND ON READING FURTHER, BEFORE TOUCHING ANYTHING.  The term does
more than charge the orbit: magneticLossFraction splits the skipped
orbits' energy into an E1 and an M1 hazard, each divided by its own
photon reference, and the checkpoint envelope identity is built on that
split.  Removing the term therefore removes the M1 PHOTON channel, not
just a line in a budget.  And the two mechanisms cover different parts of
the trajectory: the torque acts only while an orbit is mechanically
integrated, the formula acts over every skipped orbit.

THE DECIDING MEASUREMENT.  The fraction of a cascade that is mechanically
integrated, i.e. where the torque can act at all:
  measured orbits (checkpoints) / total revolutions.
If that fraction is small, the formula is the only thing covering the
rest and 136g's repair would open a gap instead of closing an overlap.

RULES, decided now.
R137a  THE REPAIR IS MADE only if the measured-orbit fraction is above
       50%, i.e. if the torque really does cover the trajectory.
R137b  If the fraction is below 1%, the repair is REFUSED and 136g's
       recommendation is withdrawn in those words, with the gap it would
       have opened stated as a number: (1 - fraction) of the M1 energy.
R137c  Either way, report the overlap the current code does have -- the
       measured-orbit fraction times the M1 share -- so the defect 136g
       pointed at keeps a size.
R137d  If the two mechanisms turn out to compute DIFFERENT powers rather
       than the same one, say so: then "double counting" was the wrong
       description and the section says which quantity each carries.

PREDICTION (mine).  The fraction is small, of order 1e-4, because the
estimator exists in order to skip orbits; so R137b applies and my own
recommendation falls.
