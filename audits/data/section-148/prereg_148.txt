WHAT 107e SAYS.  Two engine failures survived the fixes of section 107:
para at 1 r* tilt 90, failing near 0.115-0.120 r*, and ortho started at
0.35 r*, failing near 0.132-0.179 r*.  The diagnosis recorded there is
that the retarded history is a C1 cubic whose node spacing (7.8e-23 s at
the 1e-20 s floor) cannot follow an acceleration that turns over in about
1.3e-22 s, so the partner's CHARGE field jumps by order one at a node
crossing.  A finer history was named as the remedy and the cheap version
broke other things, so it stayed open.

WHAT 139 FOUND THAT BEARS ON IT.  The deep plunge at 1 r* exists only in a
narrow band of the moment radius around the model's default value: at
0.4834 r* the orbit stays above 1.00 r*, at 0.9668 it reaches 0.36 r*, at
1.9336 it stays above 0.78 r*.  Since audit 126 the same length also
softens the charge side of the moment-charge interaction, so it reaches
the very field 107e says jumps.

THE TEST.  Run both 107e configurations across the same moment-radius
scan, one orbit each, depth 20, 256 steps, tolerance 1e-8, and record
whether the engine completes, at which step it stops, and the deepest
separation reached.

RULES, decided now.
R148a  107e is RECLASSIFIED as the near-field sensitivity 139 mapped if
       both configurations complete the orbit at moment radii away from the
       default band.
R148b  It REMAINS an integrator defect if the failure persists across the
       whole scan, since then it cannot be a property of that band.
R148c  The deepest separation reached is reported at every point, because
       the mechanism 107e names is triggered by the depth of the passage
       and not by the radius the orbit started from.
R148d  If the failures vanish and the plunges vanish with them, the
       conclusion is that the failure is DOWNSTREAM of the plunge -- the
       coarse history is the proximate mechanism and the near-field
       sensitivity is the cause -- and it must be stated that way rather
       than as the integrator having improved.
R148e  No model file changes.  The scan runs through the existing switch.

PREDICTION (mine).  Both failures disappear off the band, because the
orbit stops going deep enough to reach the node-crossing regime; so R148a
and R148d, with 107e reclassified rather than fixed.
